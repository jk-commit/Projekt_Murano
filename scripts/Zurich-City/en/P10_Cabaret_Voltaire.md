## 📍 KERN

This tiny place changed modern art. During the First World War, Switzerland was neutral, and Zurich filled up with artists, writers and revolutionaries from all over Europe who'd fled the war.

On the fifth of February 1916, the writer Hugo Ball and the performer Emmy Hennings opened a club night here and called it Cabaret Voltaire. What happened on that stage was wild: poems made of nonsense sounds, costumes made of cardboard, noise instead of music. They called it Dada. Their message: the world has gone mad with war, so why should art make sense? As an art lover, this is one of my favourite places in the city.

Now look up the lane, to number fourteen. Two weeks after the cabaret opened, a Russian exile moved in there with his wife: Vladimir Lenin. He lived here until April 1917, then took a train back to Russia and started a revolution.

Did Lenin ever pop in for the Dada show? Nobody knows. But I love the idea: in one little lane in Zurich, one group was trying to blow up art, and another man was planning to blow up a whole empire.
