## 📍 KERN

This is the Raht-house, Zurich's town hall, built between 1694 and 1698. And it's literally built into the river: the Limmat flows through two arches underneath. Politicians with their feet in cold water. Some voters think that's exactly right.

It was the seat of the old city republic until 1798. Today the parliament of the canton of Zurich and the city council still meet here. And in Switzerland, they meet a lot, because people vote on nearly everything, several times a year.

[[MED-03 FULL]]
Look along the river at the big, fancy houses. Those are guildhouses. In 1336 a man called Rudolf Brun teamed up with the craftsmen, kicked out the old ruling families and gave power to the guilds. He became the first mayor. For centuries, the guilds ran this city: bakers, butchers, boatmen, merchants.
[[MED-03 CALLBACK]]
The grand houses along the river are the guildhouses of the craftsmen who took power with Rudolf Brun in 1336.
[[/MED-03]]

[[SOC-01 FULL]]
The guilds still exist, and once a year they show off: at Zex-eh-loy-ten in spring. They parade through town in historic costumes, then burn a giant snowman called the Berk on a bonfire. The faster his head explodes, the better the summer. As a man from Basel, I find this deeply Zurich: even the weather forecast is a public event.
