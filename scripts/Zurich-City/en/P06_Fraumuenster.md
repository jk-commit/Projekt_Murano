## 🔊 ANFAHRT

Ahead of you is a wide square and a church with a slim green spire. Walk over to the church entrance.

## 📍 KERN

This is the Frow-mewnster, the "minster of the women". And for once, the name tells you exactly who was in charge.

In 853, King Louis the German founded an abbey here for his daughter Hildegard and other noble women. The abbess wasn't just a nun. She had the right to mint Zurich's coins, and for a long time she was one of the most powerful people in town. A woman running the city's money, in the Middle Ages. Not bad.

[[REF-01 CALLBACK]]
That ended with the Reformation you've heard about: the abbey was dissolved, and the city took over.
[[/REF-01]]

[[ART-01 FULL]]
And now the reason I come back again and again: the windows. In 1970 Marc Chagall finished five huge stained-glass windows in the choir, blue, green, red and yellow, like painting with light. On the side there's another giant window, nine metres high, by the Swiss artist Augusto Giacometti, from 1945. If the church is open, go in. Sit down for two minutes. Let the colours do the work.
[[ART-01 CALLBACK]]
Inside, there are more famous windows: five by Marc Chagall from 1970 and a huge one by Augusto Giacometti. If it's open, step in and let the colours do the work.
[[/ART-01]]

As a former chef, I can tell you: presentation matters. Chagall understood that better than any restaurant I ever worked in.
