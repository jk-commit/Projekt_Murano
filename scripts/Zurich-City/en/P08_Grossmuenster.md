## 🔊 ANFAHRT

You can't miss this one: two big, square towers with little domes on top. Head for the main door of that church.

## 📍 KERN

This is the Grohss-mewnster, Zurich's landmark. Those two towers are on every postcard.

[[MED-02 FULL]]
The legend goes back to Roman times. Two Christians, Felix and his sister Regula, were beheaded near the river. Then, the story says, they picked up their own heads and walked up the hill to this spot to be buried. Centuries later, Charlemagne was out hunting, and his horse knelt down right over their graves. So he founded a church here. That's the legend. Historians say the building you see was built around 1100 to 1220. Either way, Felix and Regula are still the city's patron saints.
[[MED-02 CALLBACK]]
This is where the city's headless patron saints, Felix and Regula, are said to be buried, the ones from the legend.
[[/MED-02]]

[[REF-01 FULL]]
And this is where Zurich turned Protestant. In 1519 a priest called Huldrych Tsving-lee started preaching here. Within a few years Zurich broke with Rome: images and statues came out of the churches, monasteries were closed, and the city became one of the centres of the Reformation in Europe.
[[REF-01 CALLBACK]]
This was Tsving-lee's church, where the Reformation you've heard about began in 1519.
[[/REF-01]]

And if you've got the legs, climb the Karlsturm, the tower named after Charlemagne: 187 steps to one of the best views in town. Look for the stone emperor sitting on the tower. He's a copy. The original, from the 1400s, sits in the crypt, safely out of the rain. Even emperors need a roof over their heads.
