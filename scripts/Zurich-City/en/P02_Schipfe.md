## 📌 STANDORT-CHECK (nur wenn P01 oder P09 auch im Anfahrts-Radius ist)

You're on the Shipf-eh, down by the river. The park on the hill above you and the town hall on the water are separate stops.

## 📍 KERN

This is the Shipf-eh, one of the oldest corners of Zurich. Fishermen, boatmen and craftsmen lived here, right on the Limmat.

[[ROM-01 CALLBACK]]
Below the Roman customs post on the hill, this was where the Roman village sat, close to the water.
[[/ROM-01]]

Look at the plaque on the river wall. It remembers Felix Manz and other Anabaptists. They were radical reformers who believed only adults should be baptised. The city council saw that as a threat to order. On the fifth of January 1527, Manz was taken out onto the Limmat by boat and drowned, right here in the middle of the city. His mother is said to have called to him from the bank to stay strong.

[[REF-01 CALLBACK]]
That happened only a few years after Zwingli's Reformation, which you've heard about. Even reformers had limits to how much reform they tolerated.
[[/REF-01]]

Later, this lane is linked with a happier story. Protestant refugees came to Zurich, first from Locarno in the 1500s and then French Huguenots. They brought silk know-how with them, and for centuries Zurich was one of Europe's big silk cities. A city that drowned dissenters also grew rich thanks to refugees. History is rarely tidy.
